# Evidence wording

One source of truth: do not copy rules here; read them.

- Domain terms: [`_docs/team/pm.md`](../../../_docs/team/pm.md) (the "Domain terms that must be used precisely" line).
- Grounding, source labels, redacted extracts, AI-draft labelling: [`_docs/product/spec.md`](../../../_docs/product/spec.md), section Features (facts and quotes, F8 assessment) and section LLM features.

The links work from a clone. If the skill was copied elsewhere, open the files from the repository root.

## Apply

Rewrite the text so that it follows these four rules (quoted from the sources above):

1. A fact not found is **"No public evidence found"**. Never "does not have", "is not certified", "has no" or any
   other statement that the thing does not exist.
2. A source is labelled **"Supplier's own claim"** or **"Third-party source"**, never "official", "verified" or
   "reliable" on their own.
3. A quote with an omission is a **"Redacted extract"**, each omission marked `[…]`.
4. A statement written by the AI is labelled **"AI draft, pending your validation"** until the buyer decides.

Keep the buyer in charge: the AI proposes, the buyer decides. Cite the fact the statement rests on; if there is
none, name the missing evidence instead of asserting.

## Example

Draft: "The supplier does not have ISO 9001 and its site claims to be GDPR compliant."

Rewritten: "No public evidence found for ISO 9001. GDPR compliance: Supplier's own claim (the supplier's website)."
An assessment sentence built on these two facts carries the label "AI draft, pending your validation".

## Check before returning

- No negative claim about the supplier remains.
- Every claim shows its source type; every redaction shows `[…]`; every AI sentence is labelled.
- Identifiers stay in English whatever the UI language; user-facing wording follows the spec's labels exactly.
